**Eleven of nineteen — 58% — are noise.** Six describe nothing real; five are correct and not worth
anyone's time. Nothing reached critical, and nothing reached project-threatening.

That is the first measurement of what `evaluate`'s output is *worth*, as distinct from whether it is
*true*, and it is a worse number than precision alone suggested. Rounds 2 and 3 found roughly half the
sampled findings confirmable; this says that even among the true ones, most would be closed without
action.

**Read with the caveat that these ratings are not blind** (design §23.3). The reviewer had the severities
and recommendations in front of them because they were simultaneously judging the report. So this is
agreement-with-context, not the blind precision `spotcheck.py` produces, and it may not be quoted as the
same kind of evidence.

## What the reviewer found in the artifact — three real defects, all mine

**`streaming.md` overclaims, and its invariant is wrong.** The document and `STR-002` both say the final
`Prediction` is yielded after the stream. It is conditional:
`include_final_prediction_in_output_stream` (`dspy/streaming/streamify.py:35, 141, 205`) suppresses it in
the normal listener case. Verified — the reviewer is right. A state diagram caption and a table row both
assert something the code does not do.

**The coverage claim is technically true and misleading.** `README.md` says 283 of 283 files covered, but
`experimental/` (which supplies `Citations`) is described nowhere and `predict/flex/` is not identified.
A glob claims them; no document discusses them. This is exactly the assigned-versus-described gap issue
#17 exists for — and the artifact quoted the coverage number without the completeness caveat that
`archagent status` prints beside it.

**`ADP-002` is false.** It calls `format`/`parse` pure functions, while adapter subclasses are
automatically wrapped with callbacks.

## What the reviewer found in `evaluate` — one archagent defect, fixed

**Severity was displayed without saying it is mechanical.** The caveat lived inside `if flagged:`, so a
run where nothing was marked for investigation printed its findings with HIGH and MED severities and
never said what those words mean. dspy's run had 65 findings and zero flagged, so the reviewer read
exactly that report and scored `finding_restraint` 2 of 5 on it. The caveat now prints with the findings
and a test pins the ordering.

Three more, recorded and not yet acted on:

- **Recommendations are template advice, not code-specific changes** — the largest single reason
  `finding_actionability` scored 2. "Split this subsystem along its internal seams" does not name a seam.
- **Co-change findings name no shared symbol or commit**, so a maintainer must redo the analysis to act.
  This is the concrete form of the `unstable-interface` weakness rounds 2 and 3 left unresolved.
- **"keeps forcing" overstates four-to-six co-changes**, and one `hardcoded-endpoint` finding recommended
  configuring a deliberately inert test URL as though it were deployed infrastructure.

`finding_coverage_honesty` scored **5 of 5** — the one part of the report that did its job, naming the
capped list as 10 of 12, the skipped cross-service family and the reason, and the history-discipline
caution.

## What round 5 cost, and what it bought

Four archagent bugs, three of them found before a human read anything:

1. `source_paths = ["."]` resolved **no modules** — every structural signal silent, `check` passing
2. `_resolve_ref`'s `lstrip("./")` mangled any path under a dot-directory
3. a scoped `forbid-pattern` generated `./dspy/**`, which ast-grep silently ignores — the rule matched 0
   of 154 sites and reported **PASS**
4. the #28 rename leaked into `tests/rubric.py`, scoring a correct artifact 0.00 on "Artifact is
   enterable"

None of the first three was findable on archagent, paperless, wardrowbe or fastapi-template: all four use
a nested source path. The fourth was invisible to the CI gates because the rubric is evaluation code
rather than shipped code.
